I teach computer science, mathematics, critical literacy, and the foundations of machine learning and AI at further education level in Dublin. I also run AI literacy workshops for teachers and parents. Most of what I make starts as something I needed for a class, and then it grows into something that I hope proves useful for other teachers and learners.

Students can find this year's class pages at the top right under “Current classes and workshops”.

## What I am working on

Each of these began as something I needed for a class and could not find. Every tool I tried was missing something different, so I built my own, and each one grew from there. Everything runs in a web browser and the code is public.

### dewlab

[dewlab](https://deweydex.github.io/dewlab) is where my students learn programming, mathematics, databases and web development. I wanted them to work through a module without downloading anything or juggling files. It is also an experiment in how to write technical content in a humanistic way, inspired by John Dewey's idea that people learn by doing something and then reflecting on it. Each lesson puts an explanation beside code that runs on the page. [dewsharp](https://deweydex.github.io/dewsharp) does the same for C#. ([Code on GitHub](https://github.com/deweydex/dewlab))

### dewmath

[dewmath](https://deweydex.github.io/dewmath) is a diagnostic maths test for the start of a class. It shows where the gaps are, and it also helps me judge whether a learner's language level means they need extra support, because a wrong answer can come from the mathematics or from the wording. A student can answer "I don't know" or "I don't understand" and say how confident they are, and the class report shows which misunderstandings are common. ([Code on GitHub](https://github.com/deweydex/dewmath))

### AI literacy workshops

These workshops are for teachers and parents. I wanted to dispel myths and misinformation, and to do more than teach someone to use a tool. The resources help students and teachers stay critical and sceptical of AI tools, and they build new habits of mind based on a shared understanding of the limits of the tools, the hype around them and their context. [Read about the workshops](ai-literacy/).

### dewnote

[dewnote](https://deweydex.github.io/dewnote) is a simple editor in the cloud for dewlab. I was frustrated with Word and found ordinary text editors too clunky. Nothing needs to be downloaded to a computer. It is still being built. ([Code on GitHub](https://github.com/deweydex/dewnote))

### Tutorials for the web and databases

These smaller sites supplement dewlab in my classes. They let students explore HTML, CSS and SQL in a browser and give them a friendly place to start. They are also a good way in for teachers who want to use some of my material. [See each one, with links to the live sites](web-and-database-tutorials/).

## About

I love learning, and it is the thread through everything I have done for the past fifteen years. I teach computer science, mathematics and the foundations of machine learning and AI in Dublin. The most interesting work always seems to happen where the technical fields meet the humanistic ones.

### Reading

On my shelf are [Becky Chambers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Becky_Chambers), [Musa Okwonga](https://www.musaokwonga.com), [Adrian Tchaikovsky](https://www.adriantchaikovsky.com), [Douglas Adams](https://www.douglasadams.com) and [Terry Pratchett](https://www.terrypratchett.com), and in German it holds [Walter Moers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Moers), who wrote *The 13½ Lives of Captain Bluebear*. I also like [Yanis Varoufakis](https://www.yanisvaroufakis.eu) on economics and [Sarah Bakewell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Bakewell) on philosophy.

My thinking about teaching comes from [John Dewey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dewey) and [Maria Montessori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Montessori), and from more recent people who tried to change schools in practice. They include [Margret Rasfeld](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margret_Rasfeld), who wrote *Schule im Aufbruch*, [Dennis Littky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Littky) and [Elliot Washor](https://www.bigpicture.org) of Big Picture Learning, [Ken Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Robinson_%28educationalist%29), [Michael Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Apple) and [Patrick J. Finn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy_with_an_Attitude), whose book *Literacy with an Attitude* has stayed with me for years.

Most of my earlier teaching materials belong to the organisations I made them for. The ones on this site are mine, and teachers are welcome to use them.
