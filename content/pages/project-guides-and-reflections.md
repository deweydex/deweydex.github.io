These pages explain the things I find myself repeating to students, and the reasons behind how I teach. They are attempts, and I keep revising them. Several date from 2024, and I plan to rewrite them. Until I do, they live in the archive of my earlier site, which is why each page carries a banner saying so.

### Guides for learners

- [How to learn programming](old/Teaching/guides/how-to-learn-programming/) explains what programming involves, how to approach a problem you do not know how to solve, and what to do when you are stuck.
- [How to learn mathematics](old/Teaching/guides/how-to-learn-mathematics/) gives practical advice for people who find mathematics abstract or frightening. It begins with my own history with school mathematics.
- [How to learn computer science](old/Teaching/guides/how-to-learn-computer-science/) describes what computer science is and how to approach it step by step.
- [Learning mathematics through programming](old/Teaching/guides/learning-math-through-programming/) is for learners with a complicated history with mathematics. Writing code makes an idea concrete, so a symbol becomes something you can run and change. Teachers can use it to see why I teach the two subjects together.

### Reflections for teachers

- [How I think about teaching](old/Teaching/guides/how-i-think-about-teaching/) sets out the ideas behind my courses.
- [How I recommend learning to learn](old/Teaching/guides/how-i-recommend-learning-to-learn/) is a short note that I have not yet expanded.
