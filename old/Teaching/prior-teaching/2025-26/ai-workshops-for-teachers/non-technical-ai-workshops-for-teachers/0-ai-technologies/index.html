Understanding AI Technologies: A Comparison

Four Approaches to Information Access

Search Engines Pure LLMs (No Internet) RAG Models (Your Documents) Hybrid Models (Search + Docs)
How Output Is Generated Indexes web pages; returns ranked links Generates text by predicting next tokens from training patterns — Combines search, generation, and your documents—routes unpredictably
Expected Outputs Links with snippets Fluent, coherent text — Mixed: citations, generation, or both
What Success Looks Like Finds authoritative sources quickly Creates novel content; explains clearly Answers grounded in sections of your specific documents Combines current web info with your documents
What Failure Looks Like Poor sources ranked highly; ads; filter bubbles Confident hallucinations; outdated info — Unclear which approach used; false synthesis
Human Metaphor Mute librarian with call numbers—points but says nothing Person in locked room who read extensively years ago — Person in room with your book AND access to the mute librarian
The Promise "I'll find relevant links (maybe with ads)" "I'll always answer fluently and confidently" — "I'll find sources and use yours—but you won't know which"
Key Limitation Cannot create or reason—only retrieves No current info; cannot verify; confidence ≠ accuracy Limited to small sections of your included documents Unpredictable routing; unclear sourcing

Note: Most modern AI chatbots (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) are hybrids. They route queries differently based on the request, making their behavior unpredictable. You often cannot tell which approach was used for any given response.

About These Handouts

These five handouts work together to help educators understand AI tools critically and use them effectively. The sequence builds from foundational understanding through critical evaluation to practical application.

1. Understanding LLMs

Foundational explanation of how LLMs work, introducing key concepts and distinguishing LLMs from search engines and RAG systems. Emphasizes that fluency does not equal accuracy.

Best used as: Introductory reading before working with AI tools professionally

2. Understanding Student AI Use Beyond Detection

Reframes student AI use through a hermeneutic of generosity, identifying three contexts (language struggles, material struggles, time pressures) that suggest different pedagogical responses.

Best used as: Discussion starter for faculty meetings or professional development

3. Educational Prompting Examples

Five detailed examples of effective prompts for educational tasks with explanations of why each works.

Best used as: Reference guide when crafting prompts for teaching materials

4. Understanding AI Tool Limitations

Interactive exercise developing critical evaluation skills by identifying which prompts challenge different AI architectures.

Best used as: Workshop activity to build the hermeneutic of suspicion

5. Comprehensive Prompting Guide

Detailed guide introducing the four-component framework (Goal, Format, Tone, Context) with practical examples and official documentation links.

Best used as: Primary reference for developing systematic prompting methods

Suggested Sequence: Start with 1 for foundational understanding, then use 4 as a group exercise to develop critical evaluation skills. Move to 2 for pedagogical framing about student use. Then turn to 5 and 3 for practical application as reference materials.
