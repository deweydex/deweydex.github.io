Media and critical literacy

How to read a source, a news story or an argument with care, and how to say what you found.

Literacy here means more than reading words. It means asking who made a piece of media, who it is for, what it leaves out, and how the way it is framed changes what its facts mean. I teach these questions in courses on research and study skills and on communication, where students practise them on real stories, real sources and their own writing.

A good example is a project I set about the banana. The banana looks too ordinary to study. A learner chooses one of four frames: the industry and its economics, who gains and who loses from the banana trade, the damage that growing one crop on a huge scale does to the land, or the politics and colonial history behind the phrase "banana republic". Each frame turns up different facts that are all true. The learner finds that the choice of frame changes what a reader takes away.
